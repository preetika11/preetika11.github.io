About Me

I’m a Ph.D. student in the

  • Mountain Hydrology Research Group
    • at the University of Wyoming, specializing in snow hydrology, remote sensing, and geospatial data science. My research spans the cryosphere–agriculture continuum and is driven by a passion for using data to solve water resource challenges.

    My doctoral work focuses on simulating snowpack dynamics in the western U.S. using SnowModel, validated with field-based SWE and GPR-derived snow depth observations. I also assess the feasibility of retrieving SWE using L-band InSAR (UAVSAR) across various terrains and forest conditions.

    I’m building an open-source interactive map in Google Earth Engine to visualize SWE retrieval feasibility, assisting researchers and water managers in identifying suitable areas for L-band InSAR use.

    I hold an M.S. from Auburn University, where I used X-ray CT scanning and ArcPy automation to study nutrient and water retention under conservation agriculture systems.

    Tools & Skills: UAVSAR, Landsat, ArcGIS, GEE (JavaScript & Python), SnowModel, Python (NumPy, Pandas, GeoPandas), R, SAS.

    I aim to empower agriculture and water-reliant communities by combining satellite data, modeling, and AI—ensuring healthier soils and resilient snow-fed water systems in a changing climate.
